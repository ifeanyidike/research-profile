Child Development & Therapy Studio
==================================

Source map reviewed 30 October 2026. Original lessons and fictional exercises; external providers own their materials.

C01 — Choose your role, goals and learning path
Prerequisites: None
Path: Shared foundations
Study assignment: Read the discipline/role introductions and qualification guidance. Use the OT workshop only for participation context; compare caregiver understanding with formal professional preparation.
Video coverage/access: Watch only the named short NHS modules on these cards. The full multi-video workshop is not assigned. The examples provide educational context, not individual assessment or a prescribed programme.
Videos:
  CroydonHealth — Introduction to: Building Independence in Everyday Activities — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dc05W-B2qDI
    Assignment: Watch the whole introductory OT clip for everyday participation context. It does not define every therapy profession or qualification; read the role/qualification material and native path guide.
Readings:
  WHO — Caregiver Skills Training introduction (programme scope: ages 2–9) — https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240048836
    Assignment: Read the introduction’s audience, aims and programme structure. WHO CST here targets families of children aged 2–9 with developmental delays/disabilities; it does not supply a 0–18 therapy curriculum or professional qualification.
  HCPC — approved professional programmes — https://www.hcpc-uk.org/education/approved-programmes/
    Assignment: Read the named article or specified sections: HCPC — approved professional programmes. Focus on roles, scope, caregiver education and use the native worked example to check your interpretation.
Practical: Make a learning-goal table for two fictional adults: a parent preparing for an appointment and a student exploring an OT career. Give each three appropriate goals, one boundary and an evidence artefact they could produce without involving a child. Select your own learning path in the interface.
Independent assessment: A website promises “become a qualified speech, occupational and behavioral therapist in one weekend”. Write a careful appraisal covering training, assessment, supervised practice, regulation and the difference between education and a credential.
Pass criteria: 1. Give distinct caregiver and professional learning goals with scope boundaries. 2. Challenge the weekend qualification claim using accreditation, supervised assessment and regulator questions. 3. Explain why an attendance certificate does not establish professional authority.
Source notes: Educational study, not diagnosis or an individual treatment plan. Use fictional cases only. NHS materials describe local services; check your own local routes. WHO CST is primarily for ages 2–9, not an adolescent treatment programme. Professional pages require discipline-specific training and supervision. Videos may provide introductory context rather than the full professional lesson.

Pass criteria: 1. Separate observed actions from tentative interpretations in the toddler case. 2. Include hearing, context and changes in skills in neutral referral questions. 3. Respond supportively to loss of skills with timely qualified advice and no diagnosis.
