V01 — What a voice system actually does
Prerequisites: None
Path: All learners
Study assignment: Read the Stanford scope/prerequisites and HF course introduction. Watch the Edinburgh recognition overview as one component example, then draw the wider system boundary from this lesson.
Video coverage/access: These are the named video assignments for this topic. Watch in order and use the topic-specific study assignment to distinguish introductory context from the full reading and practical requirements.

  Edinburgh speech.zone — Overview 1 Of 5 — https://speech.zone/courses/speech-processing/module-7-speech-recognition-pattern-matching/videos/overview-1-of-5/

Practical: On paper, map a fictional library telephone assistant. It may read opening hours and propose a book reservation. Draw six boxes: capture, ASR, dialogue, reservation tool, TTS and transport. Give each box one input, one output and one possible failure. Mark the point where a user must approve a reservation. Use no accounts, recordings or provider calls.
Independent assessment: Design the same map for a museum information kiosk that must never change bookings. Identify two components you can simplify, two failures that remain, and the evidence needed before claiming it works for visitors with different accents.
Pass criteria: 1. Map capture, ASR, dialogue, tool, TTS and transport with inputs and failure points. 2. Remove write authority for the museum kiosk and explain two remaining failures. 3. Separate a paper design from evidence of accent coverage or product performance.
