A guide to keep nearby

Help a small moment become a conversation.

General parent education for preschool communication. Choose comfortable everyday opportunities; this is not an individual treatment prescription.

Five things to try

  1. Notice an interest. Watch what your child is doing before choosing words or changing the activity.
  2. Join gently. Copy a safe action with your own item, or make one comment. Do not require eye contact.
  3. Offer useful words. Say a short, natural message connected to the moment, such as “The car’s going”.
  4. Leave room. Pause comfortably. A gesture, action, sound or word can be a reply. Continue if there is no reply.
  5. Respond and respect an ending. Meet needs without requiring speech. Stop or change direction when your child is done.

An invented example

Child: points at a red car and says “car”.

Parent: “Red car.” Responds to the message and offers the car if that fits.

Child: rolls it without saying more.

Parent: “It’s going!” Joins the play. There is no demand to repeat “red car”.

If your child stays quiet

Supply a useful word yourself, accept the message they can communicate and continue warmly. Do not keep asking “What is it?” or “Say it”. No immediate spoken answer is required.

If it becomes difficult

Use fewer words, offer help, reduce distraction or choose another time. Never make food, drink, comfort, toilet access or needed help conditional on talking. Keep existing communication aids available.

A sample week for the adults

This is a way to organise learning, not a therapy dose or deadline. Repeat what is comfortable. Skipping a day is not failure.

Opportunity One adult skill
Monday: familiar play Watch, then join the child’s interest.
Tuesday: repeat the play Make a comment instead of asking a quiz question.
Wednesday: a comfortable game Leave space for an action, sound or gesture.
Thursday: a favourite book Stay with a picture the child enjoys.
Friday: two real options Accept a clear choice or a refusal without requiring a word.
Weekend: repeat Choose what was most comfortable. Discuss one thing the adults learnt.

Questions for a professional conversation

  • What should we know about understanding, spoken language and speech sounds?
  • Has hearing been assessed? A familiar response does not establish all aspects of hearing.
  • Can you demonstrate one suitable home strategy and an easier version?
  • What other ways of communicating can help now?
  • Which changes should prompt a review? How can we access it?

If a child loses words or other skills they previously used, contact a health professional promptly. Do not wait to complete this guide. Use local emergency care for an immediate medical emergency.

See CDC advice about concerns or loss of skills.
